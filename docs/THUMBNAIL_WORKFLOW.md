# Automatic thumbnails

For each finished clip, run the existing auto-export cover workflow. Use the clean final cut and sample the opening, middle, and payoff. Rank frames by relevance to the editorial hook, sharpness, recognizability, expression, crop fit, and room for text. Skip blank cards, blinks, transitions, credits, and competing subtitles. Confirm identities using credits and context. Review the shots around the chosen frame to make sure the image represents the clip.

Draft three accurate hooks and choose the clearest. Use two to five words on one or two lines, in large bold ivory type, with dark backing as needed. Apply the approved branding for the destination. Examples include MEET BIRD CLAY, MEET THE CLAY FAMILY, and MEET THE CAST. For ensemble pieces, choose a group shot or a clear lead with supporting cast visible.

| Destination | Design canvas | Check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| YouTube landscape | 1280 × 720 | Keep important content out of the duration badge area. |
| YouTube Shorts | 1080 × 1920 | Check frame-selection options and central crop. |
| Instagram Reels | 1080 × 1920 | Check full cover and profile grid. |
| TikTok | 1080 × 1920 | Check profile crop and overlays. |
| Snapchat | 1080 × 1920 | Check the selected destination and its cover controls. |

Pick the best frame per destination. Reuse it when the same composition works. Verify current controls for the upload route. Export a separate image when supported. For frame selection, log the exact timestamp and use a separate titled export over moving footage if needed. Keep the clean master intact. Label unsupported custom covers as previews.

Preview at phone thumbnail size, then adjust crop, shorten the headline, or try the next candidate until the subject and text are readable. Log the frame timestamp, headline, dimensions, filename, and application method. Flag unclear identity, unusable crops, and unsupported cover behavior. Follow the repository's rights and publication rules.
